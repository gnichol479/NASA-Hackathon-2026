# Last Signal narration sources

Factual narration is grounded in NASA or NASA/JPL material. The project uses short paraphrases; it does not reproduce NASA copy verbatim. The countdown, room layout, assembly activity, and compressed rocket movement are educational presentation elements, not a recreation of the actual timeline.

## Opportunity: mission and launch

- [NASA Science: Opportunity](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mer-opportunity/) — mission purpose, 90-sol plan, instruments, 45.16 km distance, last communication, and key dates. NASA distinguishes the Jan. 24, 2004 Pacific local landing date from Jan. 25 UTC.
- [NASA/JPL: Newly Launched Opportunity Follows Mars-Bound Spirit](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/newly-launched-opportunity-follows-mars-bound-spirit/) — July 7, 2003 liftoff at 11:18:15 p.m. EDT from Cape Canaveral Air Force Station, Delta II, and initial trajectory toward Mars.
- [NASA/JPL: Opportunity mission ends](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/mer/nasas-opportunity-rover-mission-on-mars-comes-to-end/) — dust storm, hematite evidence, extended mission, and end-of-mission context.
- [NASA Science: Meridiani Planum](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/meridiani-planum-2/) — hematite concretions (“blueberries”) and the evidence for ancient groundwater.
- [NASA Science: Opportunity’s final Mars images](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/opportunity-rovers-final-mars-images/) — 354-image Pancam panorama collected May 13–June 10, 2018, and the final panorama before the dust storm.
- [NASA/JPL: Opportunity's steepest climb](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/rover-takes-on-steepest-slope-ever-tried-on-mars/) — the rover reached a 32-degree tilt on March 10, 2016, a Mars-rover record at that time.
- [NASA Science: Mars Exploration Rover 10-year lithograph (PDF)](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/44694_MER10-YearAnniversaryLithograph.pdf) — 90-day/sol mission context, hematite concretions, and jarosite in the Meridiani rocks.
- [NASA/JPL: Opportunity’s mission on Mars comes to an end](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-opportunity-rover-mission-on-mars-comes-to-end/) — final commands, mission duration, distance, and recovery attempts.
- [NASA/JPL: Perseverance sets off-world driving record](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-perseverance-sets-off-world-record-for-total-distance-driven/) — on Oct. 11, 2026, Perseverance passed Opportunity’s 45.16 km total.
- [NASA: MER-B environmental review (PDF)](https://netspublic.grc.nasa.gov/main/MER%20DEIS%20July%202002.pdf) — the planned Delta II 7925H launch sequence for MER-B, including six ground-lit and three air-lit solid motors.

## InSight

- [NASA Science: InSight](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/insight/) — launch date/time, Atlas V-401 vehicle, Vandenberg launch site, landing, and science objective.
- [NASA/JPL InSight launch press kit: facts](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/press_kits/insight/launch/facts/) — launch window, vehicle, and launch site.
- [NASA/JPL InSight launch press kit: mission overview](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/press_kits/insight/launch/mission/) — launch sequence and the southbound trajectory from Vandenberg.

## Ingenuity

- [NASA Science: Ingenuity Mars Helicopter](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-2020-perseverance/ingenuity-mars-helicopter/) — Ingenuity's technology-demonstration purpose, Mars flights, and first powered, controlled flight on another planet.
- [NASA: United Launch Alliance Atlas V Rocket Launches with NASA's Mars Perseverance Rover](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/united-launch-alliance-atlas-v-rocket-launches-with-nasas-mars-perseverance-rover/) — joint Perseverance and Ingenuity launch on Atlas V 541 from Cape Canaveral on July 30, 2020.
- [NASA: NASA, ULA Launch Mars 2020 Perseverance Rover Mission to Red Planet](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-ula-launch-mars-2020-perseverance-rover-mission-to-red-planet/) — launch time and mission context.

## Scope note

NASA is the source for mission history and science facts. The fictional briefing-room setting, interactive assembly order, controller instructions, visual mock-ups, and abbreviated countdown/flight timing are clearly part of the experience rather than claims about NASA's actual facilities or exact launch operations.
